## Three clues, not one

This repo had been working from the mirror clue alone. There are three, and
they are cumulative:

1. **George Sand's hidden cryptography.** The apocryphal Sand / Alfred de
   Musset exchange: read every other LINE, in full, and the alternate lines
   form a separate message.
2. **Mirror writing.**
3. **"El Salvador" is a clue.** Notably, "Bitcoin Magazine | El Salvador" is
   printed rotated 90 degrees in the sidebar of *every* page of the piece.

## The article is confirmed as the right one

"OVERDOSE" is Keiser's column in the **El Salvador edition = Issue 24**, and
Issue 24 is the issue the 20 BTC is hidden in. The scans in this repo are the
correct pages.

## What this exposed in our own coverage

The George Sand reading — **full alternate lines concatenated** — had never
been generated or swept. `column_cipher.py` only ever took the first or last
WORD or LETTER of alternate lines. Verified: the odd-line concatenation appears
in none of the swept corpora. Now generated by `gen_sand.py` (825 readings) and
swept: 1,513,875 addresses, 0 hits.

Reading the output settles the literal version of the cipher: both alternate
readings are grammatically broken at every join —
*"Bitcoin was not a reaction | Really? Yes."*,
*"the white paper | arose in the global unconscious"*,
*"They are the sum of | around that time"*.
So the article was not written so that alternate lines flow. Keiser's
invocation of Sand is likely thematic ("a key hidden in plain text") rather
than the literal alternate-line device.

Note also that `gen_sand.py`'s boundary-continuity metric reports INCONCLUSIVE
rather than a verdict, because its own positive control (the natural reading)
scores +0.0 sd against a line-shuffled null — this article's lines break at
phrase boundaries, so even genuine continuity leaves no trigram signal across a
join. A metric that cannot detect the continuity it is designed to detect must
not be used to rule anything in or out.
