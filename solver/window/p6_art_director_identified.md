# P6: the art director has a name (2026-09-19)

`what_keiser_controlled.md` argued that P6 — *who chose the $100 image* — is the
highest-value open question in the project, because it decides whether ~2.3M
derivations were aimed at the art department's clip art. P6 was blocked on not
knowing who to ask.

## The name

**Duff Ferguson — Art Director, Bitcoin Magazine (BTC Inc.), since September
2017.** Confirmed by his LinkedIn and by Bitcoin Magazine's own coverage of him
(*"Bitcoin Magazine's Art Director To Auction Guy Fawkes Painting For Bitcoin"*,
April 2021, which describes him as art director of BTC Inc. at that date).

September 2017 → the El Salvador issue shipped Fall 2021. **He was art director
for Issue 24.** Portfolio at `duffferguson.com`; the Behance gallery
(133310833) that this repo already had covers the *cover* only, so it is a
different and narrower artifact.

This is the first time the project has had a person to address the design
questions to, rather than an abstraction called "the designer".

## Status of the portfolio PDF

A candidate portfolio document was surfaced at
`duffferguson.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/HG-GovtDebt-redact.pdf`.
**This container cannot read it** — `duffferguson.com` returns 403 at the
egress proxy, as every other external host does, and the search index does not
carry its contents. Nothing about its contents is established here.

Two cautions before anyone builds on it:

1. The filename suggests a piece about **government debt** for a client
   abbreviated `HG`, "redact" meaning a redacted portfolio copy. It may not be
   Bitcoin Magazine work at all, and may have no bearing on Issue 24.
2. A portfolio PDF is a designer's *showcase*. Even if it contains Issue 24
   spreads, it will not necessarily carry asset credits.

## What would actually be decisive, in or out of that PDF

Asked of the document, or of Ferguson directly:

| question | why it decides something |
|---|---|
| Does the OVERDOSE spread (pp. 73–79) appear? | establishes he designed it |
| Is the $100 banknote credited to a stock agency (Getty / Shutterstock / Adobe Stock asset ID)? | **closes the serial branch permanently.** A stock ID means the serial is the agency's, not Keiser's |
| Did Keiser supply any image or asset for the spread? | the converse — if he supplied the note, the serial is his and the branch is live |
| Who shot the photograph of Keiser in the field? | the composite's provenance |
| Did Keiser supply any string, glyph or artwork to be set verbatim? | the one channel a setter could guarantee would survive typesetting |

That last row is the real prize, and it is worth asking even if the banknote
answer is boring. `what_keiser_controlled.md` establishes that Keiser controlled
the character stream and essentially nothing visual. The single exception would
be **an asset he handed the magazine to reproduce as-is** — and only the art
director knows whether one exists.
